## In-video checkpoints

- 0:53: With k = 3, the neighbours are mango, lemon, mango. What is the prediction? (answer: Mango)
- 1:47: Where is the model most likely to be wrong? (answer: Near the border between groups)

```text
# Video 6: How your AI decides: nearest neighbours

[00:00:00.000] (Intro) MyCampusView Academy. AI for Young Builders, video 6. How your AI decides: nearest neighbours.
[00:00:11.500] How did your model decide? It used one of the oldest and simplest ideas in AI, called k nearest neighbours.
[00:00:21.300] Imagine every example as a dot on a map. Here, fruits are placed by sweetness and size. Mangoes gather in one corner, lemons in another, because similar things sit close together.
[00:00:37.800] A new fruit arrives. The model measures how far it is from every example, picks the three closest, and lets them vote. Two mangoes and one lemon. Mango wins!
[00:00:53.500] The letter k is just how many neighbours get a vote. With two groups, people pick an odd number like three or five, so the vote can never tie. And changing k can change the answer, so it is worth experimenting.
[00:01:10.700] Your drawing model did exactly this. Each drawing became two hundred and fifty-six pixel numbers, which is a dot in a space with two hundred and fifty-six directions. We cannot picture that, but the maths works the same way.
[00:01:28.800] Where does the model go wrong? Deep inside a group, every neighbour agrees, so it is easy. Near the border between groups the neighbours are mixed and the vote is close. Adding examples near the border helps the most.
[00:01:47.400] (Outro) Let us recap. Similar examples sit close together. The k nearest examples vote. Mistakes happen most near the border between groups. Next: Testing: is your AI any good?
```
