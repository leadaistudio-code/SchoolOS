## In-video checkpoints

- 0:38: A picture is 10 pixels wide and 10 tall. How many numbers in black and white? (answer: 100)
- 0:55: How many numbers describe one colour pixel? (answer: Three: red, green and blue)

## Narration script

Clear, friendly, slow Indian English for Classes 5–8. Pronounce: AI (A-I), k-NN (K-N-N), RGB (R-G-B), Bhashini (BHAA-shi-nee).

```text
# Video 4: How computers see: pixels

[00:00:00.000] (Intro) MyCampusView Academy. AI for Young Builders, video 4. How computers see: pixels.
[00:00:10.500] You see a smiley face. A computer sees a grid of numbers. Let us find out why.
[00:00:19.500] Zoom in far enough on any digital picture and you find tiny squares called pixels. In a black-and-white picture each pixel can be stored as a number: one for black, zero for white. So this smiley face is really sixty-four numbers.
[00:00:38.700] Grey pictures use a number from zero to two hundred and fifty-five for each pixel. Colour pictures use three numbers per pixel: how much red, green and blue light to mix. That is called RGB.
[00:00:55.300] The numbers add up fast. Our smiley is sixty-four numbers. A small handwritten digit, twenty-eight by twenty-eight, is seven hundred and eighty-four. A tiny colour photo, a hundred by a hundred, is thirty thousand. A phone photo is millions.
[00:01:16.900] Why does this matter? Computers can only calculate with numbers. An AI never sees a cat. It sees numbers, and learns which patterns of numbers usually mean cat. Similar pictures have similar numbers. Remember that, because your model will use it.
[00:01:40.500] (Outro) Let us recap. A picture is a grid of pixels. Each pixel is stored as a number. Colour pixels have red, green and blue numbers. Next: Train your first AI model.
```
