## In-video checkpoints

- 0:41: What usually causes bias in an AI? (answer: Unbalanced or unfair training data)
- 1:19: Why check accuracy for each group? (answer: An overall score can hide a group that does badly)

## Narration script

Clear, friendly, slow Indian English for Classes 5–8. Pronounce: AI (A-I), k-NN (K-N-N), RGB (R-G-B), Bhashini (BHAA-shi-nee).

```text
# Video 12: Fair AI: when data is unfair

[00:00:00.000] (Intro) MyCampusView Academy. AI for Young Builders, video 12. Fair AI: when data is unfair.
[00:00:11.200] An AI learns exactly what its data teaches it, including the unfair parts. This is called bias.
[00:00:20.900] Imagine a face-unlock AI trained on eighteen adults and only two children. It works well for adults, ninety-six percent, but only fifty-five percent for children. Nobody meant it to be unfair. The data simply had too few children.
[00:00:41.300] This has really happened. Researchers found face recognition systems that made far more mistakes on darker-skinned faces. Voice assistants struggled with Indian accents. Companies improved them by collecting more varied data.
[00:01:00.400] Here is a sneaky problem. This model is ninety-five percent accurate overall, which sounds great. But for group B it is only sixty percent. An overall score can hide unfairness, so always check each group.
[00:01:19.400] So how do we make AI fairer? Collect data from every group. Balance the numbers. Test each group separately. And always ask: who might this AI leave out?
[00:01:35.900] (Outro) Let us recap. Bias means AI treats some people or things unfairly. Unbalanced data makes a model bad at the smaller group. Check accuracy for each group, and balance the data. Next: Responsible AI: privacy, deepfakes and you.
```
