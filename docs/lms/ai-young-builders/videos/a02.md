## In-video checkpoints

- 1:15: In machine learning, who works out the rules? (answer: The computer, from labelled examples)
- 1:37: What comes straight after labelling the examples? (answer: Training the model)

## Narration script

Clear, friendly, slow Indian English for Classes 5–8. Pronounce: AI (A-I), k-NN (K-N-N), RGB (R-G-B), Bhashini (BHAA-shi-nee).

```text
# Video 2: Rules or learning? How AI learns

[00:00:00.000] (Intro) MyCampusView Academy. AI for Young Builders, video 2. Rules or learning? How AI learns.
[00:00:11.800] There are two ways to make a computer smart. One is very old. The other powers almost every AI you use today.
[00:00:22.900] The old way is rules. A person writes exact instructions, like: if the temperature is above thirty degrees, switch on the fan. The program takes an input, follows the rules, and gives an answer.
[00:00:40.300] Now try writing rules for: is this a photo of a cat? Cats can be big or small, orange or striped, sleeping, jumping or hiding behind a curtain. You would never finish writing the rules.
[00:00:56.600] So machine learning flips it around. You give the computer lots of examples, and the right answers, called labels. It finds the patterns itself and builds a model. Then you give the model something new, and it makes a prediction.
[00:01:15.700] Machine learning always follows five steps. Collect examples. Label them. Train a model. Test it on examples it has never seen. And then use it. If the test goes badly, go back and collect better data.
[00:01:37.300] So which should you use? Ringing the school bell at nine o’clock needs one simple rule. But reading handwriting, or spotting spam, is messy and keeps changing. That is where learning shines.
[00:01:54.200] (Outro) Let us recap. Normal programs follow rules a person wrote. Machine learning finds the rules from labelled examples. The steps are collect, label, train, test, use. Next: Data: features and labels.
```
