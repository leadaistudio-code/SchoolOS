```text
# Video 8: Decision trees: AI that asks questions

[00:00:00.000] (Intro) MyCampusView Academy. AI for Young Builders, video 8. Decision trees: AI that asks questions.
[00:00:11.600] Remember the game where you guess an animal by asking yes or no questions? A decision tree is an AI that plays that game.
[00:00:22.100] It starts with one question at the top, called the root. Has feathers? Yes: it is a bird. No: has fur? Yes: mammal. No: fish. The answers at the ends are called leaves.
[00:00:42.300] The skill is choosing good questions. Has feathers splits out the birds perfectly. Is it brown is useless, because birds, fish and mammals can all be brown. Computers build trees by trying every question and keeping the one that splits best.
[00:01:02.800] Now test your tree on tricky animals. A whale lives in water but is a mammal. A penguin is a bird that cannot fly. A bat flies but is a mammal. A tree that is perfect on its training animals can still trip over these.
[00:01:22.800] Decision trees have a superpower: you can read every step. That means people can check them and spot unfair questions. So they are used where decisions affect people, like deciding which patients a nurse should see first.
[00:01:41.300] (Outro) Let us recap. A decision tree asks yes-or-no questions. Good questions split examples into pure groups. You can read and check every step. Next: Teaching AI to read: words and feelings.
```
