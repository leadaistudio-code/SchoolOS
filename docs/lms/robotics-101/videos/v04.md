## In-video checkpoints

- 0:58: What happens when the sensor is above a black surface? (answer: Most of the infrared light is absorbed, so little comes back)

## Narration script

Clear, friendly, slow Indian English for Classes 5–8. Pronounce: Arduino (aar-DWEE-no), L298N (L-two-nine-eight-N), PWM (P-W-M), infrared (in-fra-red).

```text
# Video 4: How an IR sensor sees the line

[00:00:00.000] (Intro) MyCampusView Academy. Robotics 101, video 4. How an IR sensor sees the line.
[00:00:10.600] How can a robot see a black line? It uses invisible light called infrared. Your TV remote uses the same kind of light.
[00:00:22.200] Each sensor module has two small parts. One is an infrared light, which shines down at the floor. The other is a receiver, which measures how much light bounces back.
[00:00:36.900] Over a white floor, most of the light bounces back. The receiver sees a lot of light, so the sensor says: white.
[00:00:47.400] Over a black line, the black surface soaks up most of the light. Very little comes back, so the sensor says: black.
[00:00:58.000] The module turns this into a simple digital signal, either HIGH or LOW. On many modules, black gives HIGH and white gives LOW. Some are the other way round, so you will test yours in lesson nine.
[00:01:14.300] Each module has a small blue knob. Turning it changes how much light counts as white. Hold the sensor about five to ten millimetres above the floor and turn the knob until the LED changes exactly when you cross the line.
[00:01:32.000] Our robot uses two sensors, one either side of the line. When both see white, the line is safely between them. Watch the sensor dots as the robot drives.
[00:01:49.000] (Outro) Let us recap. White reflects infrared; black absorbs it. The module turns that into HIGH or LOW. Adjust the knob to calibrate. Next: How motors and the motor driver work.
```
